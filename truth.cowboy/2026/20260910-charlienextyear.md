# Charlie, One Year Out

I have pondered this journal entry for several months,
and now we're already past the one year anniversary of the assassination.

## Charlie Who?

I really didn't know anything about Charlie Kirk before his fatal shooting.
And then came a flurry of news, mixed with the expected social commentary,
elevating Kirk to martyr status in some circles, amplifying the us-vs-them
divide in the country where I live.

As I saw videos of Charlie engaging with people, I was impressed by his
"let's walk through the facts" style of engagement. As a race (the whole
human race, that is), we need more of that. Instead we jump onto the emotion.
But emotion should be a gauge rather than a guide. We choose our opinion
before engaging reason. We should instead consider the logic (or lack!)
in whatever the other person is saying. Many people would step away
when debating with Kirk, likely recognizing their own lack of logic.

## The Christian Nationalist ... NOT

In my lifetime, and prior, there has been a growing tug-of-war between
public morality and libertine lifestyles. There are many English speakers
who fail to distinguish "libertine" from "liberty".

I was not always a Christian, and I'm no saint in the parochial sense,
but I have come to see the freedom in righteous living. I mean this in the
Judeo-Christian sense: sexual purity, sure, but also fair treatment of others,
even mundane things like common courtesy and following traffic laws.
(Though I confess the speed limit can be a challenge.) It's probably
more clear to call me a "Christ follower", but see below for
clarification of what it means to be a Christian.

The "tug" goes against this, especially regarding sexuality.
It's as if any suggestion of restraint imposes on the liberty of some other.
But pushing for morality does not make me a "Christian Nationalist".
Fact: moral purity is healthier.

I do vote for leaders and representatives who support right living,
and I find stronger support from committed Christians and Jews.
But supporting traditional marriage does not make me a Christian Nationalist,
nor did sharing the Gospel make Charlie Kirk a Christian Nationalist.

## He Wasn't Always That Way

I suspect Charlie became a Christian\* at a young age, but when he began
his public career he did not push faith. In fact, "early Charlie" was not
particularly pro-Christian nor pro-life. Young Charlie Kirk became
impressed with the political and economic positions pronounced by the
Republican party and initially spoke up for those conservative values.
He didn't even finish college but went straight into activism.

Over time, something changed. Maybe someone clarified the work of Jesus Christ.
In any case, his Christian faith became part of what Charlie promoted
in his public encounters. Salvation in Jesus Christ is so much more
profound than politics. But the reaction from the audience can be similar
and it's easy for the media to combine all things controversial or uncomfortable.
To be fair, it does not appear that Kirk made much effort to isolate
the topics from each other. That gets confusing: some would think that
to be pro-American one must be Christian or that to be Christian
one must be politically conservative. These are orthogonal topics.

This is the motivation for this journal entry:
There's a big difference between the Gospel of Jesus Christ
and American conservative politics, and Charlie Kirk was not always a "preacher".

## What is Christian?

\*Scripture says "... if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus
and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead,
you will be saved." (Romans 10:9). Elsewhere Jesus Himself says,
"... unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God." (John 3:3).

There are countless people in the country where I live who presume a Christian
identity who do not know this. I was such. When confronted with the true
"Gospel", I reacted against it. That's a common reaction. Maybe it's pride,
because "... for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, ..."
(Romans 3:23). Indeed, many churches no longer mention this (I attended one),
perhaps hoping to not offend. But then, "For the wages of sin is death,
but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our lord." (Romans 6:23).
There is hope! Why not preach THIS?!?

And that's what Charlie Kirk eventually did.
But he evidently DID NOT say that one must be Christian to be an American.
He was not a "Christian Nationalist".

## Conservative

As a Christian, my main motivation is what I read in Scripture.
What that leads to might be "conservative" or could just as easily be "liberal".
Whether something is liberal or conservative depends on the societal context.
In the tug-of-war of loosening morals in the US, following Godly direction
naturally looks conservative.

Conservatism in and of itself is a fine goal, but it's not an end itself.
Its value is merely in *conserving* something good or healthy or valuable.
200 years ago (plus and minus several decades) many Christians fought to
abolish the slave trade. That would have been the *liberal* position of the time.

Charlie was politically conservative from the get go.
Later, he was "conservative" on topics related to Biblical discussion
(being pro-life, etc). More important, he was clear about genuine Christianity,
which made him a hero with many in the born again crowd. Kirk's proclamation
of the Gospel is for everyone, liberal or conservative.
