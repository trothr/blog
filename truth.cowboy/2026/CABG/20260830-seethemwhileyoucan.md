# See Them While You Can

We had one of my dearest friends over for dinner last month. <br/>
He called, asked if he could come visit, we said "come for supper".
He felt bashful about perhaps inviting himself to a meal,
but some relationships are just that easy and we prevailed upon him.

Since the heart surgery, and even before,
the outpouring has been incredible. This journal entry
describes just a little bit of the amazing flood of support.

## Friends, Neighbors, Family

I've been fighting high blood pressure for years. I said something
about it to one of the cardiologists, feeling like a failure.
He responded that heart disease sneaks up on people.

I remember when transitioning from out-patient testing to the ER
thinking, "life changes just this quickly". And so it does!

Susan called the kids. They hit the road immediately
and were with us at the hospital the very next day.
Thank's, God, for family!

Two friends were in the area and stopped by right away.
Thanks, God, for friends!

The church put us on their prayer list.
Then when I was released, they provided a meal train.
Thanks, God, for the Church.

## Visitation

At the hospital, we received more visitors than I can count.
I wish I could thank them all. When we got home, we continued
receiving visitors, and got phone, txt/sms, and email well wishes.

I'm so thankful that I had the corrective surgery.
I'm so thankful that I "woke up" after the procedure.
WE DON'T KNOW how much time we have. We don't know how much time
others have (those we might visit with).

The older we get, the more likely that we'll encounter some infirmity.
Naturally, there are some in my circle that I'm anxious to see in person
because I don't know how long the opertunity will persist.
