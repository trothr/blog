# Let Me Know You're There

It's been a long while since I've journaled *here*.
Life took a dramatic turn two weeks ago (it was Wednesday
and today is Sunday, so not quite two full weeks).
I'm writing this as a triple-bypass open heart surgery post op patient.

I'll tell more about THAT story (CABG curgery) in a separate journal entry.
Suffice for now that there's an understandable lot of fear and uncertainty.
By the time of the third heart catheterization, the kids had arrived,
which was refreshing and encouraging.

We're back home now.
Returned to the house around 3:30 PM on Saturday 2026-08-08.

## The God of All Comfort

Back to that heart cath ...
Transport came to fetch me. I asked Susan and the kids if they wanted
to head down or just wait in the private room. They said they'd be in the
post procedure room and meet me there. So then when I got to pre-procecure
prep, two very attentive young nurses got me ready. "Where's your family?".
Oh the irony. "They'll meet here in this room when we're done.". 
"Sounds good! Perfect.".

They wrapped up what they needed to do and left the room. <br/>
I saw alone.

Normally no big deal to be alone, but I was already looking ahead to 
open heart surgery in a day or three. That team would soon STOP MY HEART
for several hours. I began to get a little fearful. If I did not wake up,
I'd be with God. I knew that. Even so, I really hoped that I would not leave
Susan to have to bury a second husband or Chelle and Martin without their
one remaining biological parent.

"God, I just need to know that you're with me."

His response was instant.

"I will never leave you nor forsake you."

The peace that surpasses understanding flooded my soul. <br/>
Thanks, Lord!

I've known Hebrews 13:5 and Deuteronomy 31:6 and 8 for years.
But I was not searching my gray matter for that line.
All I did was privately call out, "please help", and He did!
God used language I know well to tell me, "I'm here and I'm not going anywhere.".

As I write, ten days after the event, it's still difficult.
My chest is very sore post-op and the emotion is intense.
It's all *good* emotion, but still extreme.

This is so beautiful. <br/>
God knows what we need and He meets us there.

And we're not done!

## My Cup Runs Over

I was content. <br/>
I was calm, peaceful, at full rest. <br/>
I figured they'd roll me into the "cath lab" shortly.

Then in walked Susan, Chelle, and Martin. "Hi, Dad!".

SWEET!!! <br/>
There was the icing on the cake.

I'm an "enneagram 2". In another measurement they call me "sanguine".
I'm all about relationships. I love my people: Jesus with skin on, so to speak.

But this is what God does ... He takes care of what we need and then
blesses us even more.

My whole body has endured a reboot. Brain too.
I'm not even sure that my writing here makes sense to y'all. Hope so!

2026-08-09, 2026-08-10, sdg
