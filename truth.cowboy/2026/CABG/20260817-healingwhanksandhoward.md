## They Did Not Know

Sometime after the "Houston, we've had a problem." part,
I realized they didn't know if they'd make it back. Well, duh!
But seriously, we who watch/review the story from perspective of the
21st century simply cannot put ourselves fully into that 1970 reality.
A similar thing happens with WWII. (The allies did not know that they'd
defeat the Nazis.)

When you're looking at open heart surgery, it's like that. <br/>
YOU DON'T KNOW that you're going to wake-up after the operation.
In 2026, it's likely. But "likely" and "certain" are far apart.
So there I was prior to Tuesday the 4th anticipating this procedure. <br/>
I DID NOT KNOW.

## Eternity

This is not the first time I've had eternity in my face.
Late 2019, my first wife Marilyn had developed pancreatic cancer.
Nobody used terms like "terminal" or "stage four" with us. That's probably
just as well. But we knew it was serious. And then her liver tipped downward
and took her kidneys and that shut everything down. It hurt. Bad.

Since then I have remarried. I met Susan in a program called Grief Share.
We became friends. Ironically, the dreaded COVID 19 pandemic turned out to be
a blessing for Susan and me because we lived 45 minutes apart and were forced
to go online. Our Grief Share group became a highlight of each week.

End of Summer 2017, Susan lost her first husband Ronney. He'd also been through
bypass surgery, so a lot of my cardiac saga was a trigger. But she did great!
Sadly, Ronney had a condition such that his bypass grafts also became blocked.
He was gone even faster than Marilyn.

We are Christians. We believe that God created us; we know that we (as a race)
rebelled in the Garden of Eden, but that God provided redemption: Jesus Christ
taking the penalty on that cross. So ... life is real, eternity is real, Heaven,
hell, all of it. I'm no better than anyone else, but should I NOT wake up
from that open heart surgery, I will be with God in Heaven. Amen!

I did not want to leave Susan to have to bury another husband. <br/>
(At least, not this year, please.) So I prayed.

## Legacy

I also did not want to leave Chelle and Martin.
(See above about them having lost their mom.)

(BT/DT ... I still need elders too!)

What is left to do? What really matters?
I am so blessed! I have the best job that I have ever had.
There's "legacy" code which the team must maintain. (Oh and it gets *forensic*,

After Susan lost Ronney and I lost Marilyn, God led us to each other.
Neither of us expected that life could be so good, especially after tragedy.
But it is! So if there's a legacy we want to leave, *that's it*.
Susan's coaching minstry is focused on "healing, hope, and new horizons".
